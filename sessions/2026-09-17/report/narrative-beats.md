## Per match, the gap separates the winner — and the evidence is weak

Order the seven by pinglamb's advantage: **+3.90 (m2, yachi) · +13.47 (m3, yachi) · +16.91 (m1) ·
+21.17 (m6) · +30.83 (m5) · +36.82 (m4) · +44.36 (m7)** [C007]. yachi's two wins are the two
smallest; nothing between 13.47 and 16.91.

**Say the power or do not say the result.** Two wins among seven arrange this way by chance with
probability 1/C(7,2) = **1 in 21**, against 1 in 120 for 08-19's 3 of 10 and 1 in 126 for 08-25's
4 of 9. So the count goes to 3 of 12 and the three are not equally powered — this one is about six
times weaker. It is not 09-11's 1 in 7, which the corpus files as degenerate, so the claim is
minted; but the 1-in-21 travels with it.

Two guards that carry over unchanged: **it is not a sign test** (pinglamb's attack per piece is
higher in all seven matches, yachi's two wins included), and **the cut point is not a constant**
(it falls between 13.47 and 16.91 here, between 8.21 and 10.32 on 08-25, between 0.72 and 11.53 on
08-19).

m7's +44.36% is the largest per-match gap the corpus has, past 08-19's +40.67%.
